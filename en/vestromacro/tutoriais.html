Tutorials

Learn to use Vestro

Short videos showing Vestro in a sample spreadsheet and system: from the first click on Record to copying data from one program to another. The same videos are in the Tutorial button, at the top of the app.

Record a task

To record, do the task the usual way. Here, an order in the spreadsheet is marked as paid: a click on the cell, the word “paid” and Enter. Each click and each key becomes a box in the workflow, and each arrow keeps the time between them.

Step by step

  1. Open a workflow and click Record (or press F9).
  2. Vestro hides and shows the Recording badge at the top of the screen.
  3. Do the task as usual: here, click the cell and type paid.
  4. Press F9 again to stop.
  5. Done: each click and each key became a box, and the arrows keep the time between them.

Play

The “Mark paid” workflow types “paid” and presses Enter. With the first cell selected and 3 repetitions, Vestro marks three orders in a row. By default, it gets out of the way while playing, so the keys go to the right program.

Step by step

  1. Get the program ready: here, the first cell that will receive the text.
  2. In Repeat, choose how many times. Speed makes it faster or slower.
  3. Click Play (or press F8). Vestro gets out of the way while the macro runs and comes back at the end.
  4. Want to see the box that is running? In the playback options, turn off Minimize and use F8 with the program in focus.
  5. To stop halfway, press Esc. The ∞ repeats until you stop.

Edit the workflow

After recording, everything is adjusted right in the flow. Here, the letters of “paid” become a single Text action, the wait gets its own time and the part that marks the order becomes a loop of 3 rounds.

Step by step

  1. Click a box to see and change its details in the drawer below.
  2. Select several with Shift + click and delete with Delete.
  3. Drag an action from the panel (here, Text) to the right spot and write the text.
  4. Click an arrow to change the wait before the next action.
  5. Select a stretch and press Ctrl+G to turn it into a loop. Choose the number of rounds.
  6. Play it to check. Mistake? Ctrl+Z undoes it.

See where the mouse clicks

Each yellow box is a mouse click, with its position on the screen. The eye shows that point with a crosshair on top of everything. With several boxes selected, Vestro shows all the clicks numbered, in order.

Step by step

  1. Hover over a mouse box and click the eye.
  2. A crosshair appears at the exact point of the screen where the macro will click.
  3. Select several (Ctrl+A selects all) and press E.
  4. The clicks show up numbered, in the order they happen.

Move a click

The system was updated and the Export button moved: the macro started clicking on empty space. In the click drawer, “Click on the screen” hides Vestro and waits for you to click the new spot. There you can also change the mouse button and the kind of click.

Step by step

  1. Play it: the button moved and the click lands on empty space.
  2. Click the mouse box and, in the drawer, Click on the screen.
  3. Vestro hides: click the new spot of the button.
  4. The position changes right away. In the drawer you can also change the mouse button and the kind of click.
  5. Play it again: now the click hits the button.

Memories: copy and paste between programs

The recorded Ctrl+C becomes a Copy action, which can store the text in a memory (M1 to M5). The Paste action uses the memory you choose, even with other copies in between. In a loop, Vestro moves the name and Tax ID of the three customers from the system to the spreadsheet.

Step by step

  1. The recorded Ctrl+C becomes the Copy action. In the drawer, in Store the copied text in, choose Memory 1 for the name.
  2. Do the same with the Tax ID, in Memory 2.
  3. In the Variables tab, name the memories: Customer and Tax ID.
  4. In each Paste, choose the right memory in What to paste.
  5. Play it: on each round, Vestro copies both fields and pastes both into the spreadsheet.

Constants: the text that repeats

Constants are texts that do not change: an e-mail, the company name, a department code. You write it once in the Variables tab and use it in any Paste. The text changed? Change it in one place only.

Step by step

  1. In the Variables tab, write the constant: a name and the text.
  2. In a Paste action, choose the constant in What to paste.
  3. Choose Paste (Ctrl+V) or Type letter by letter.
  4. Play it: the constant's text goes into each row.
  5. The text changed? Change it in the Variables tab: every action that uses the constant changes too.

Lists: a different value in each round

A list keeps several values, one per line. The List action types the current value and, on each round of the loop, moves on to the next one. Here, the customer list fills the spreadsheet column.

Step by step

  1. In the Variables tab, click New list and write one value per line.
  2. In Moves on at each round of, choose the loop: each round uses the next value.
  3. Drag the List action into the loop.
  4. Play it: each round types a customer from the list.

Functions: build the text on the fly

A function builds the text at paste time. Here, it creates the order number from the loop round: ORD-001, ORD-002, ORD-003. The result shows up while you write the formula.

Step by step

  1. In the Functions tab, click New and give it a name.
  2. Build the formula with the buttons: text in quotes, & joins the parts and LOOP is the round number.
  3. The result shows up right away. Save.
  4. In the Paste action, choose the function in What to paste.
  5. Play it: on each round the function calculates again.
